A home PV installation, measured

Solar, night consumption, and whether a battery pays

Four reports, built from six years of five-minute solar history and fifteen-minute smart-meter data. They were written in order, and each one changed the answer the previous had reached — including one that found a hardware fault which invalidated an entire phase. Every figure on these pages is computed from the data beside it.

Phase 1

The solar window

When solar generation is actually possible, derived from six years of five-minute history rather than from an almanac. Panels wake about three minutes before geometric sunrise, so the boundary is fitted to when they really start and stop, and the fit is what makes every later page's definition of “night” empirical.

Phase 2superseded

Night consumption and battery sizing

The first attempt at sizing a battery, from PVOutput's own consumption channel. Kept because the method is sound and the comparison is instructive — but that channel was later found to have lost about a quarter of the household load from December 2022, so its figures understate the night and the battery. Read the meter page instead.

Phase 3 & 4

Night consumption, measured at the meter

The same question rebuilt on smart-meter data, which measures what actually crosses the grid connection, then priced against a real 2027 tariff. Contains the fault that invalidated phase 2, the winter wall that bounds any battery answer, and the capacity the euros actually justify.

Phase 4b

Is the battery worth buying?

A different question from all of the above: not what size, but whether to buy at all, for hardware already chosen, against leaving the money invested elsewhere. The manufacturer states two lifetimes and they disagree about the answer, which is the honest headline.
